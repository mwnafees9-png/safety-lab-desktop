Trust & Security

Certification-grade data, protected end to end

Safety Lab Aero holds the safety and airworthiness evidence for uncrewed and clean-sheet aircraft programs. This page describes how that data is isolated, encrypted, governed, and — when the time comes — provably destroyed.

Last reviewed: July 2026  ·  Questions: waqas.nafees@safetylabaero.com

Security posture, in one line

Every application table enforces row-level security in the database itself; data is encrypted in transit and at rest; the deterministic engine — not the AI — owns every safety number; and account deletion issues a signed certificate of destruction. Controls marked LIVE are in production today; those marked ROADMAP are in progress and dated on request.

What's in production today

Tenant isolation & access control

Separation is enforced by the database, not just the application — so a bug in the UI can't leak another customer's data.
  • Row-level security on every table — all application tables run RLS; access is filtered per row.
  • Workspace & project scoping — membership and role are checked in the database on every read and write.
  • Least-privilege roles — owner / admin / editor / reviewer / member, evaluated server-side.
  • No shared tenancy in the client — the browser only ever holds a public, anon-scoped key; privileged keys never reach it.

Encryption

  • In transit — TLS 1.2+ on every connection to the application and API.
  • At rest — AES-256 encryption of the database and stored objects.
  • Managed key handling — encryption keys are managed by the hosting platform, not embedded in the app.
  • No secrets in the browser — AI and privileged operations run behind a server-side proxy.

Authentication & account security

  • Email + password with verification — accounts must confirm their address before use.
  • Two-factor authentication (TOTP) — optional authenticator-app second step, enforced at sign-in once enabled.
  • Microsoft Entra ID (Azure AD) SSO — work-account sign-in, restricted to an approved-tenant allowlist enforced server-side.
  • Idle auto–sign-out — sessions lock after 20 minutes of inactivity, every session, no exemption.
  • Forced reset on recovery — a password-reset link cannot become passwordless inbox access; a new password is required.
  • Password minimums & safe error handling — no account-existence disclosure on failed sign-in.

AI safety & data handling

In a domain where "the model made it up" is disqualifying, the AI is deliberately kept away from the numbers.
  • The engine owns every number — safety math, allocations, and robustness claims come from a deterministic core that carries its basis and citation. The AI never authors a number, a requirement, or a verdict.
  • AI drafts within guardrails — the assistant is scoped to drafting and explanation; its output is reviewable and never silently written into the safety case.
  • Server-side proxy — AI calls route through a controlled gateway; the client never holds a model provider key, and requests are logged for audit.
  • Customer content may be used to improve our models — content you submit, including your corrections to AI drafts, may be used to train and evaluate Safety Lab Aero’s own models. Content from projects you flag as export-controlled is excluded entirely. We never expose one customer’s content to another, and you can opt out at any time. A zero-data-retention addendum with our AI subprocessor is being finalized (see roadmap).

Data residency & export control

  • US-region infrastructure — primary data stores run in a US region (US-East).
  • ITAR-aware egress — an egress control governs where flagged content may be sent for processing.
  • Subprocessor transparency — the full list is below and kept current.
  • GCC High & on-prem — separate deployment tiers for defense programs are on the roadmap.

Data lifecycle, audit & integrity

  • Self-service erasure — an account owner can permanently erase their account and all analysis data.
  • Certificate of destruction — every erasure issues a signed certificate with a SHA-256 manifest of what was deleted.
  • Tamper-evident sign-offs — review sign-offs are chained with a verifiable hash chain; the chain can be re-verified on demand.
  • Audit logging — workspace and review activity is recorded for accountability.
